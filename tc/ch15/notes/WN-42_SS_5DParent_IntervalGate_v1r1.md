# Scale Space: finite-interval covariant parent, exact massive light mode, and boundary/spectral decision gate — corrected working edition v1r1

**Date:** 10 November 2026  
**Working edition:** v1r1, additive repair of the equation (7) boundary coefficient and explicit single-interval/orbifold normalization.  
**Status:** Noncanonical research candidate; author adoption NOT sought or presumed.  
**Scope:** An explicit compact-interval completion of the *proposed* five-dimensional Einstein–scalar–Dirac–Maxwell parent, compared against the accepted finite common-metric SS-DYN v8 [doi:10.5281/zenodo.23862296](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23862296). No change to SS-DYN, SS-MAT [v1, doi:10.5281/zenodo.23863555](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23863555) or published material.  
**Outcome:** PASS for a conditional finite-interval 5D background and an *exact* protected massive Dirac mode at tree level. HOLD for a healthy, unique or adopted physical SSC parent: boundary terms are tuned and negative-tension, radion stabilization is absent, light tensor/gauge KK states emerge as the interval grows, and quantum protection of the constitutive functions is unknown.

## 1. Why this is the next gate

The preceding [WN-41](WN-41_SS_Parent5D_Decision_v1.md) demonstrated that the SS protected weight `sech^4(kappa y)` can be generated by a genuine covariant 5D Einstein–scalar background, but found (i) gapless tensor and gauge continua on the complete scale line, (ii) a `1/14` projected mass-source mismatch for a constant 5D mixing mass, and (iii) sensitivity of gauge localization to a uniform bulk kinetic counterterm. This work replaces only the *domain* by a finite interval and solves the same-source mass/profile problem exactly. The essential question is whether the repaired model is genuinely nearer a parent, rather than a regulator that introduces new unsolved assumptions.

## 2. One declared 5D bulk action

In signature (-++++), on `I_L=[-L,L]`, take a **dynamical five-dimensional metric** with the flat-worldvolume domain-wall ansatz

\[
 ds_5^2=e^{2A(y)}g_{\mu\nu}(x)dx^\mu dx^\nu+dy^2,\quad
 \theta=\phi/\sqrt{6M},\quad M=M_5^3>0.
\]

The bulk action is

\[
 S_{\rm bulk}=\int_{I_L}d^5x\sqrt{-g_5}\left[
 \frac M2R_5-\frac12(\nabla\phi)^2-V(\phi)
 -\frac{Z_A(\phi)}{4e_5^2}{\cal F}_{MN}{\cal F}^{MN}
 +\sum_{s=\pm}\overline\Psi_s(i\Gamma^MD_M-sY(\phi))\Psi_s
 -{\cal M}(\phi)(\overline\Psi_+\Psi_-+\overline\Psi_-\Psi_+)
 \right].\tag{1}
\]

The scalar superpotential and corresponding potential are inherited *unchanged* from the preceding candidate:

\[
 {\cal W}(\phi)=6M\kappa\sin\theta,\qquad
 V=\tfrac12 {\cal W}_{\phi}^2-\frac{2{\cal W}^2}{3M}
 =3M\kappa^2(1-9\sin^2\theta).\tag{2}
\]

The **additional constitutive functions are explicit model choices** (not yet symmetry-derived):

\[
 Z_A(\phi)=\cos^4\theta,\qquad
 Y(\phi)=3\kappa\sin\theta,\qquad
 \boxed{{\cal M}(\phi)=m\sec^2\theta}.\tag{3}
\]

Here `m` is the 4D Dirac mass *input*. To make the previously implicit orbifold convention precise, take a **single physical interval** with its standard Gibbons–Hawking–York (GHY) terms and **half of each doubled/jump boundary parameter** in the actual interval action:

\[
S_{I,\partial}=S_{\rm bulk}+M\sum_{i=\pm}\int_{\partial_i I_L}d^4x\sqrt{-h_i}\,K_i-\frac12\sum_{i=\pm}\int_{\partial_i I_L}d^4x\sqrt{-h_i}\,\lambda_i(\phi),\qquad K_i=4n_i A'(y_i).\tag{3a}
\]

This **fundamental-interval normalization**, in which the physical gravitational reduction has coefficient `M∫_{-L}^L e^{2A}dy`, is equivalent at the background junction level to doubling both bulk and GHY contributions and including the full `λ_i` once. The doubled-cover action, if counted as a distinct physical volume, would also double the bulk Planck normalization; it must not be mixed with the single physical interval's normalization. In the fundamental-interval action the actual brane-action coefficient is `τ_i=λ_i/2`. Define the **doubled/orbifold junction parameters**
\[
 \lambda_i(\phi)=-2n_i{\cal W}(\phi),\quad n_-=-1,\ n_+=+1,\tag{4}
\]
with junction convention
\[
 n_iA'={\lambda_i\over6M},\qquad n_i\phi'=-\frac12\lambda_{i,\phi}.\tag{5}
\]
The convention can be checked *by variation of the background action*, not only by substitution. For a flat four-dimensional slice, `R_5=-8A''-20(A')²`; the GHY term cancels the total derivative, leaving

\[
S_{\rm red}=\int d^4x\left\{\int_{-L}^Ldy\,e^{4A}\left[6M(A')^2-\tfrac12(\phi')^2-V(\phi)\right]-\tfrac12\sum_{i=\pm}e^{4A_i}\lambda_i(\phi_i)\right\}.\tag{5a}
\]
Its endpoint variation, with outward normals `n_i`, is
\[
\delta S_{\rm red}\big|_{\partial I}=\int d^4x\sum_i e^{4A_i}\left[(12M n_iA'_i-2\lambda_i)\delta A_i+(-n_i\phi'_i-\tfrac12\lambda_{i,\phi})\delta\phi_i\right].\tag{5b}
\]
The vanishing coefficients reproduce exactly (5). For an alternative strictly one-sided action with `-∑ τ_i` and no orbifold parameter, the equations instead read `n_i A'=τ_i/(3M)` and `n_i φ'=-τ_{i,φ}`. These are the **same equations** when `τ_i=λ_i/2`; using them with `τ_i=λ_i` would be an inconsistent factor-of-two substitution. Thus the convention is now fixed at the level of the *background ansatz*; a full constrained boundary/radion calculation remains open. In the probe spinor/gauge sector impose a gauge-invariant transverse Neumann condition on the gauge field, compatible transverse-tensor Neumann condition, and chiral boundary projections that remove the unwanted zero-mode chiralities. Complete constrained boundary gravity and fermionic self-adjointness remain separate review gates.

The exact bulk solution on the interval is the unchanged restricted background
\[
 A(y)=-2\log\cosh(\kappa y),\quad
 \phi(y)=\sqrt{6M}\arcsin[\tanh(\kappa y)].\tag{6}
\]
This solves the full background Einstein and scalar equations, and (4) satisfies (5). With `t=tanh(κL)`, the **defined doubled junction parameters**, and separately the **coefficients appearing in the single-interval action**, are
\[
\boxed{\lambda_+(\phi(L))=\lambda_-(\phi(-L))=-12M\kappa\tanh(\kappa L)},\qquad
\boxed{\tau_+=\tau_-=\tfrac12\lambda_\pm=-6M\kappa\tanh(\kappa L)}.\tag{7}
\]
The original v1 printed `-6Mκ tanh(κL)` as `λ_i`, inadvertently substituting `τ_i` for the *defined* `λ_i`. Both signs remain negative. The correction changes no boundary function or junction relation. The negative sign remains a physical drawback, not an algebraic error. The central smooth wall and two negative-tension regulators are not demonstrated to define a healthy radion/gravity system.

## 3. Exact massive Dirac profile: a positive result beyond the previous gate

Take the opposite-chirality light profiles of the two bulk fermions with equal normalized transverse amplitude
\[
 f_L=f_R=\frac{e^{-A/2}}{\sqrt{H_L}},\qquad
 H_L=\int_{-L}^{L}e^{2A}dy.\tag{8}
\]
Choose conventions in which the light left and right radial Dirac equations contain
\[
 f'+(2A'+Y)f=0,
\]
while the four-dimensional mass coupling leaves the algebraic residual
\[
 (m e^{-A}-{\cal M}(\phi))f.\tag{9}
\]
On (6), `A'=-2 kappa tanh(kappa y)`, `Y=3 kappa tanh(kappa y)`, and `f'/f=kappa tanh(kappa y)`, so **both equations vanish pointwise**, not just after integration, with the choice (3). The two retained spinor chiralities form an exact four-dimensional massive Dirac field of mass `m` in the **fixed background probe Dirac equations**, with the wrong chiralities set to zero by the declared boundary projections.

The light-mode kinetic/current, Maxwell and bulk graviton normalization densities agree:
\[
 \rho_{\rm kin}=e^{3A}|f|^2={e^{2A}\over H_L},\qquad
 \rho_{\rm mass}={e^{4A}{\cal M}|f|^2\over m}={e^{2A}\over H_L},\qquad
 Z_A=e^{2A}.\tag{10}
\]
Thus the first order `1/14` mismatch of the *constant mass* alternative is removed and all direct heavy-gauge/vector-current overlaps vanish at the exact wall background. This statement covers the named projected 4D metric sources; it does not show that the complete 5D matter stress including transverse stresses, graviton/scalar constraints and quantum loops are universally protected.

**Cost:** on the complete line, `M(y)=m cosh^2(kappa y)` diverges. On the finite interval it is smooth and bounded but largest at the boundaries: `M_edge/m=cosh^2(kappa L)`. It also varies when the localizer is excited. No Ward identity or symmetry has yet been shown to require this function or to protect it against renormalization.

## 4. The exact truncation–mass-amplitude theorem

Set `t=tanh(kappa L)` and let `delta` be the fraction of the *noncompact* gravity/gauge normalization removed by truncation. Then
\[
 H_L={2\over\kappa}\left(t-\frac{t^3}{3}\right),\quad H_\infty={4\over3\kappa},\quad
 \boxed{\delta=1-H_L/H_\infty=\tfrac12(1-t)^2(t+2)}.\tag{11}
\]
The required endpoint mixing strength is `C=M_edge/m=1/(1-t^2)`. Therefore the **exact identity**
\[
 \boxed{\delta C^2={t+2\over2(1+t)^2}\ge\frac38},\qquad 0\le t<1.\tag{12}
\]
The inequality follows because `(t+2)/(2(1+t)^2)` decreases monotonically from 1 to `3/8` as `t` increases from 0 to 1. In particular `delta >= 3/(8 C^2)`. A finite bound on the local mixing relative to the 4D mass imposes a minimum discrepancy from the full-line normalization. This is not a theorem relating the mass to the UV cutoff unless a bound `M_edge <= Lambda` is additionally justified.

## 5. Finite spectral gap: positive but costly

The physical transverse graviton and gauge equations of the *covariant 5D action* with (3) have the same finite-interval Sturm–Liouville form
\[
 -\partial_y(e^{4A}u')=m_n^2e^{2A}u,\qquad [e^{4A}u']_{\pm L}=0.\tag{13}
\]
The constant mode is normalizable and massless. The positive modes have a discrete nonnegative spectrum for every finite `L`. The positive gap is **not** the original noncovariant weighted-operator threshold `2kappa`, and for increasing `L` it becomes small. The table below gives the first odd and even positive eigenvalues in units `kappa^2`. The calculation uses a 70-function odd/even Neumann Galerkin method with 1100 Gauss nodes; results were checked at 48 modes and independently using a piecewise-linear finite-element method.

|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 10% | 0.927229 | 2.1362 | 3.878055 | 8.088486 | 1.96928 |
| 1% | 1.571095 | 6.2994 | 1.027811 | 1.146434 | 1.01381 |
| 0.1% | 2.166183 | 19.5343 | 0.139966 | 0.141035 | 0.37412 |
| 0.01% | 2.747794 | 61.4048 | 0.015136 | 0.015146 | 0.12303 |

As `L→∞`, a pair of odd/even tail-localized trial functions has Rayleigh quotient `O(e^{-4kappa L})`: numerator integrates the `e^{4A}~e^{-8kappa L}` stiffness; denominator integrates the `e^{2A}~e^{-4kappa L}` norm. Thus the *positive* excitation gap approaches zero. For an exactly protected source its direct heavy overlap stays zero; a regulator/shape-induced mismatch need not remain insensitive to these light modes.

This establishes a **quantitative tension**: achieving a highly accurate noncompact normalization by taking `L` large produces very light tensor/gauge excitations and requires increasingly strong mass mixing near the boundaries. It does not prohibit a finite-interval theory; it prohibits claiming the original gapped SS-DYN spectrum is inherited without further mechanism.

## 6. A permitted gauge kinetic floor remains a protection problem

Add a constant small, positive, gauge-invariant bulk term `epsilon F_{MN}F^{MN}` through `Z_A -> F+epsilon`. On the finite interval the photon zero mode **remains normalizable**, with `H_epsilon=H_L+2 epsilon L`. However the fermion source stays `rho=F/H_L`, not proportional to `F+epsilon`. With `J` the conserved light vector current, the complete direct squared heavy-mode overlap relative to its zero-mode coupling is
\[
 \boxed{R_\epsilon={H_\epsilon\over H_L^2}
 \int_{-L}^{L}{F(y)^2\over F(y)+\epsilon}dy-1\ge0}.\tag{14}
\]
It is strictly positive for `epsilon>0` on any nontrivial interval, by Cauchy–Schwarz. The light electromagnetic coupling also shifts from `e5/sqrt(H_L)` to `e5/sqrt(H_epsilon)`.

Illustration for **hypothetical** `epsilon=0.01` in normalized units: at `delta=0.001`, `kappa L=2.166`, the squared heavy-current overlap ratio is `R_epsilon=0.005526` and the light gauge coupling shifts by `-1.588%`. At `delta=0.0001`, these become `0.013197` and `-1.999%`. They are conditional response diagnostics, **not loop predictions**. The finite interval removes the *normalizability catastrophe* of a uniform floor, but does not protect charge universality/source alignment against it.

## 7. Remaining gravitational constraint and physical status

The boundary potentials in (4) are chosen to admit the background. They have negative tension at both cutoffs. The same functional boundary conditions admit a continuous family of `L`: the proper separation between the two endpoints is `2L`, so the interval size is an unfixed modulus in this tuned family. A healthy radion kinetic term, stabilization potential, gauge constraints, and backreaction must be derived. The existence of a family suggests a massless modulus but does not replace its full constrained-spectrum calculation. A Goldberger–Wise-type stabilization is a known way of fixing separations in warped models, not a mechanism already supplied by our candidate (Goldberger & Wise 1999).

The exact massive light sector is a result for a *fixed wall and metric*, not a nonlinearly invariant subtheory under arbitrary dynamical `X`, massive gauge backreaction or interactions. The chosen dielectric, transverse Yukawa and spatially varying Dirac mass are not derived by a common protecting symmetry. The earlier SS-DYN `D_{N,L}` scalar pole (`m_Q/kappa~340.936` in its particular parameter benchmark) has not been derived from this complete covariant 5D model. There is no demonstrated mapping between its proper transverse coordinate and an individual object's intrinsic Scale Space coordinate.

## 8. Scientific gate and recommendation

| Gate | Decision |
|---|---|
| Exact 5D Einstein–scalar kink background and tuned finite endpoints | PASS, model-dependent |
| Normalizable massless tensor/gauge zero modes with positive finite-mode gaps | PASS at finite `L` |
| Exact massive protected 4D Dirac profile in the fixed background | PASS with chosen `M(phi)=m sec²(theta)` |
| Full-line normalization and bounded edge mass simultaneously without tradeoff | FAIL for this exact family, theorem (12) |
| Preserve an `O(kappa)` positive KK gap as `delta->0` | FAIL for the Neumann covariant interval family |
| Healthy/stabilized boundary radion and no negative tension problems | HOLD |
| Quantum protection of dielectric, Yukawa and mass source weight | HOLD |
| Equivalence to canonical common-metric SS-DYN scalar spectrum or independent-leaf parent | HOLD |

**Decision:** This **does** make the fully covariant route more concrete: the constant-mass mismatch can be repaired at the level of full background Dirac equations, while the otherwise singular noncompact mass function is rendered finite. Nevertheless, merely compactifying does **not** select a unique or complete SSC parent. The candidate should remain noncanonical. The next truly decisive test is a constraint-solved stabilized boundary action whose source-protecting symmetry survives loops; failing that, the accepted restricted common-metric SS-DYN is the better established programme foundation.
